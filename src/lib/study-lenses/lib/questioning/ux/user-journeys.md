# Learner journeys through questioning

The user twin of the questioning family. The user this module owes a model to is
the **learner who meets the questions** — not an interface user: no screen is
modeled here (lenses own rendering). What is modeled is **progression** — how
comprehension moves through the grid, where misconceptions surface, and what
each journey demands of a questioner. The menu item is user journeys rather than
personas or wireframes because the module's user-risk is progression: the order
of what a learner encounters, what a wrong turn earns, and when help appears.
Grounding: [PEDAGOGY.md](../../../PEDAGOGY.md) (layers, SOLO, threshold
concepts, expertise reversal); the family's own laws:
[../README.md](../README.md).

A boundary, stated once: these journeys are a **design model, not a mechanism**.
Questioners never sequence, adapt, or remember (the neutrality law) — a journey
says what item data must EXIST for a consumer to build that path, never that a
questioner walks it.

## J1 — First contact: naming what is on the page

**Who arrives:** a learner early in the comprehension of a snippet —
pre-structural to uni-structural (SOLO) on its concepts; reading at the atom
level of the text-surface dimension.

**The path:** closed-register items ask "what kind of element is this?" against
single anchors. Each answer meets a verdict; feedback explains in NM vocabulary
why the answer is what it is. The learner's grid position barely moves — and
should not: many small correct namings build the uni-structural floor everything
else stands on.

**A wrong turn earns:** a verdict plus the machine's explanation — never a score
that follows them, and never a locked door (mastery display and gating are
consumer choices outside the family).

**Help appears:** inside the item — the feedback channel — not beside it.

**What this demands of questioners:** many atom/text-surface items (the cells
each item carries are what make that coverage auditable) and answer keys whose
feedback is written in NM-speak, not in "correct!" speak.

## J2 — A misconception surfaces and is repaired

**Who arrives:** a learner holding a wrong-but-working model — the classic
threshold-concept cases: a variable "contains" its name, declaration and
assignment are one event, source text and runtime are the same thing. Wrong
models produce right answers for a while; that is what makes them stable.

**The path:** an item whose distractor IS the misconception. The learner picks
the answer their model predicts — and the verdict makes the model visible for
the first time. Feedback names the machine event that the model got wrong. What
follows is liminality: floating between the old certainty and the new model.
PEDAGOGY.md's threshold-concept treatment is explicit that this floating is a
legitimate position, not failure — so the journey allows re-encounters: the same
misconception met again on new snippets, as many times as integration takes.

**A wrong turn earns:** the most valuable event in the journey — a visible
contradiction between prediction and machine. The wrong turn IS the progression.

**Help appears:** after the contradiction, not before it — help that answers in
advance would hide the model the item exists to surface.

**What this demands of questioners:** distractors authored FROM known
misconceptions (curated-bank territory: the copy is authored, the key is
machine-derived), execution-dimension items that force prediction, and a stable
re-encounter — the learner must be able to meet the same question again to test
their repaired model (the landed engine's determinism provides this; a
randomizing questioner owes its own re-encounter story).

## J3 — Crossing into the open register

**Who arrives:** a learner fluent in gradable answers — multi-structural,
comfortable at text-surface and execution — who has never been asked a question
without an answer key.

**The path:** an open-register question at a familiar anchor: "why is it written
this way?" No verdict arrives, and none ever will. The judge is now the learner
(or their human teacher); the purpose dimension — closed to any grader — opens.
The discomfort of no key is not a defect of the journey; it is the journey:
comprehension of intent and design rationale has no machine-derivable ground
truth, and meeting that fact is itself a threshold.

**A wrong turn earns:** nothing mechanical — there is no wrong; there is shallow
and deep. The Feedback-Ladder registers (open → pointed → comparative) let the
questioning sharpen without ever grading.

**Help appears:** as hints that are tool references — "the trace might help" —
Socratic pointers toward a strategy, never toward an answer.

**What this demands of questioners:** both registers sharing one grid and one
anchor system, so a consumer can place the open question AT the anchor the
learner already knows from closed items — the one-grid commitment doing its
work.

## J4 — Traversing the grid

**Who arrives:** a learner solid at the atom and block levels of a snippet,
ready to relate parts — SOLO's relational move, made on the grid.

**The path:** the same snippet, re-met at deeper cells: from "what is this
element?" (atom / text-surface) through "what happens when this runs?" (block /
execution) toward "why are the parts arranged this way?" (relation, macro /
purpose). Dimension and level advance independently — the grid is a coordinate
system, not a ladder — and a consumer plans the traversal by reading cells;
nothing in the family imposes an order.

**A wrong turn earns:** a return to a more concrete cell of the same snippet —
possible only because items at every cell coexist, so "go back down" is a
selection, not a regeneration.

**Help appears:** as the coexistence itself: the concrete items are still there
while the abstract ones are attempted.

**What this demands of questioners:** honest cell tagging (the audit surface),
coverage that spans levels and dimensions rather than pooling at atoms, and item
independence — any subset of items must stand alone, since the family guarantees
no delivery order.

## J5 — Expertise reversal: the questions fade

**Who arrives:** a learner for whom scaffolded closed items have become noise —
the expertise-reversal point where support that once helped now costs.

**The path:** the closed register recedes; the open register remains; and past
it, the learner's own questions begin — the curriculum's L3/L4 territory, where
the goal is asking well, not answering correctly. Nothing is withdrawn BY the
system: the fade is pull, not push — the learner stops reaching for keys, then
stops reaching for authored questions at all, having internalized the
questioning stance the open register models.

**A wrong turn earns:** nothing — at this point "wrong turn" has no referent;
the learner owns the judgment.

**Help appears:** only if reached for; anything pushed here would be the
expertise-reversal mistake itself.

**What this demands of questioners:** config filtering rich enough that a
consumer can thin the stream (kinds, categories, registers, levels — off by
choice, not by mutation), and open items whose questions are worth imitating —
the last thing a questioner teaches is how to ask.
