# Manifestos Direction

> The three manifesto files
> (`syllabus.manifesto.{learners,authors,community}.md`) are siblings of
> `syllabus.ontology.md` but operate at a different register. The ontology is
> reference-voice; the manifestos are prose addressed to a specific audience.

## The manifesto's job

The manifestos describe the _why_ of the course at registers the ontology can't
carry. They are:

- **Plural** — one per audience. Each says what the course is _for_ / _with_ /
  _to_ that audience. The audience cuts:
  - `syllabus.manifesto.learners.md` — addressed to learners (what the course is
    _for you_)
  - `syllabus.manifesto.authors.md` — addressed to curriculum authors, forkers,
    contributors (what the course is _for the people who teach with it or adapt
    it_)
  - `syllabus.manifesto.community.md` — addressed to partner communities,
    mentors, cohort hosts (what the course is _with you_ — community-as-making,
    not community-as-served)
- **Prose** — addressed in second person ("you")
- **Format discovers itself per audience** — no single template

## Authority is plural and the plural is wide

The principles are not Evan's _claims_ but _crystallizations of community
practice_. The community-of-origin includes:

- **Classroom community** — alumni, current students, prototype cohorts
  (Palestinian-community cohort upcoming)
- **Collegial community** — friends, mentors, co-developers
- **Partnered community** — organizations, cohort hosts
- **Discourse community** — Hofstadter, Friston, Levin, Wing, Papert, Bruner,
  Perkins, Wiley, Bakhtiar Mikhak, Malaise & Signer, Greg Wilson, Sorva,
  Cunningham et al, Karpathy, Willison, Van Merriënboer, Kirschner, …

Evan is the _scribe_ of what working-in-community has shown is true. The course
is what one careful person can do with what a community has shown them.

## The intellectual-agency through-line

(User, clauding-7) — **intellectual agency is THE META learning objective
unifying all 5 layers**. Each layer is intellectual agency at a different scale.
This is likely the manifestos' through-line:

- **Learners' manifesto**: developing your intellectual agency — _AI can't have
  your generative model for you_, and the curriculum is built to develop yours.
  Each layer (mastery / rhetoric / methodology / snippetry / philosophy) is a
  different scale at which you take agency over how you think, what you build,
  and how you decide.
- **Authors' manifesto**: building for / respecting learners' intellectual
  agency. The course is opinionated about respecting agency — that's why it
  works through experiences (non-delegable) rather than explanations
  (delegable). When you adapt this course, honor the agency-stance — don't
  replace experience with explanation.
- **Community manifesto**: depending on and amplifying agency. The curriculum is
  made _in community_ — with alumni, friends, mentors, cohort partners, the
  discourse community. The fluid-roles framing (learner / companion / teacher /
  accounta-buddy) is how agency circulates: nobody is purely one role; everyone
  is becoming.

## Operative voice tests

The manifestos should pass these:

| Test                             | Pass                                                               | Fail                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Authority**                    | "What years of practice with many people and many texts has shown" | "I believe" / "my method"            |
| **Lineage**                      | Cited as part of a discourse the manifesto continues               | Cited to credential Evan             |
| **Personal glory**               | None. No autobiographical sections.                                | Anything that centers Evan           |
| **Belgian dryness**              | Factual directness; warm without enthusiasm-overselling            | "Amazing" / "incredible" / "blazing" |
| **Lightness on the GEB lineage** | A footnote-able rhyme                                              | A structural framing                 |
| **Reign-in-GEB**                 | "WtF _builds_ intellectual confidence"                             | "WtF _requires_ it"                  |

## What's deliberately open

- **Anchor set** — don't fixate on "intellect & experience" (that was
  conversational discovery, not derived ontology). The anchors emerge through
  drafting and user direction. Likely involves _intellectual agency_
  prominently; may involve experience, community, lineage, practice — but the
  exact framing surfaces in iteration.
- **Form** — each audience may want a different shape. A learners' manifesto may
  benefit from address-first ("You arrived here with a why…"); an authors'
  manifesto may benefit from claim-first ("This course is opinionated about
  agency…"); a community manifesto may benefit from invitation-first ("We made
  this with you and for what we're all making together…"). Discover as drafting
  progresses.
- **Length** — each manifesto should be only as long as it needs to be. A short
  one is fine if it's complete.

## What the manifestos are NOT

- NOT GEB-style puzzles requiring intellectual confidence to engage — _"WtF
  builds intellectual confidence; GEB requires it"_ (user, R3)
- NOT credits rolls
- NOT marketing copy
- NOT auto-bios
- NOT defenses of human-vs-AI capacity — the stance is _proof through practice_,
  not argument
- NOT ceremony around community — community is operational (the course is made
  with the community-of-makers AND for the community-of-users AND the two are
  the same kind of relationship at different moments)

## Iterative drafting workflow

Manifesto + ontology-2 stabilize together (R3 lock — "iterative"):

1. Start with manifestos as openers / preambles only
2. Iterate ontology sections; surface manifesto fragments as they arise
3. Each pass through the ontology may surface manifesto material; each manifesto
   fragment may surface ontology gaps
4. Both files stabilize together
5. Chapters.md follows

The current state of the manifesto files (this commit): openers and preambles
only, with structural scaffolding for what each will need to say. The body
content is the next session's iterative work with the user.

## Reference: the user's voice spec (from `narrative/README.md` §25)

- **Dry base** — state ideas directly, without performance. No excessive
  enthusiasm, no motivational language, no marketing speak.
- **Middle-band playfulness** — characters can appear; historical cameos can
  have personality; a warm aside is welcome; cultural literacy can peek through.
  _Not_ full Poignant-Guide weirdness. Think: a thoughtful teacher with dry wit,
  not a comedian.
- **Cultured and quietly warm** — references (musical, historical, literary) are
  welcome when they serve. Respects the reader as an adult.
- **Honest about uncertainty** — evidence-informed but not dogmatic.
- **Belgian/European-adjacent reserve** — dry realism, unwilling to oversell.
  Not cold, not cynical. Measured.
- **Second person common** — "you" is the reader. "We" is sometimes the
  curriculum authors, sometimes the broader community of programmers.

## R4 update — TCER per-audience treatment

After the initial manifesto drafts, the user surfaced TCER as a load-bearing
framework. The integration of TCER vocabulary differs significantly per
audience:

| Audience                             | TCER treatment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Learners**                         | **NOT TOUCHED.** Learners don't need TCER vocabulary to learn. The translational nature is invisible at the learning level. `syllabus.manifesto.learners.md` stays free of TCER framing.                                                                                                                     |
| **Authors / forkers / contributors** | **Substantial section added.** Adaptation is trading-zone work in TCER's technical sense. The Evidence Tag System (🔬/📐/🧪) and reflexive-analysis discipline are introduced. Two traps named (translational imperative, pipeline misconception). Pointer to `syllabus.translational-framing.md` for depth. |
| **Partner communities**              | **Substantial section added.** Co-development IS translational research. Partner cohorts ARE trading zones (in the technical sense, not user-tests). Two traps named. Pointer to `syllabus.translational-framing.md` for depth.                                                                              |

**`syllabus.md` itself** — also NOT touched. The translational nature is for
_meta-docs only_; learner-facing prose stays free of it.

The new sibling file **`syllabus.translational-framing.md`** is where the deep
analysis lives. The manifestos point at it; they don't duplicate it.

## What to avoid

- Hyperbole ("amazing", "incredible", "blazing fast")
- False confidence ("this will definitely work")
- Sycophantic agreement with imagined student thoughts ("Great question!")
- Enthusiasm not backed by evidence
- Overexplaining — trust the reader to follow
