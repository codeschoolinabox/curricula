### R4 — TCER framework integration (load-bearing)

The curriculum is a **translational research artifact** in the technical sense
of TCER (Cole, Malaise & Signer 2023, SIGCSE — the user is first author). This
is not metaphor; it's the framework's own use case. Key claims to honor:

- **V/F operates at TWO scales**: student layer (Vibetoader/Frogrammer with
  their respective twins) AND artifact layer (**the curriculum = V** —
  experiential affordances; **`lenses/embody` = F** — engineering / technical
  affordances). Note the artifact-layer V↔F mapping flips relative to surface
  intuition (R5 correction). The two artifacts operate as **coordinated
  Translational Sprints**.
- **"We are doing the innovation process we're teaching."** The student-layer
  V/F recurses into the artifact-layer V/F.
- **Bakhtiar Mikhak's lineage**: _infrastructure IS research contribution_ +
  _engineering × physics co-evolution_. Both from the same teacher; both ground
  V/F.
- **The two TCER traps**: _translational imperative_ (over-justifying every
  change) and _pipeline misconception_ (translation is cyclical, not linear).
- **Pedagogy Wins**: when pedagogical effectiveness and research observability
  conflict, pedagogy wins. Always.

When you write or revise anything in the curriculum, ask: does this honor the
trading-zone framing? Does it preserve the curriculum's status as a coordinated
translational artifact alongside `lenses/embody`? If yes, proceed. If no,
surface the tension; don't quietly bury it.

**Learners' manifesto and `syllabus.md` stay free of TCER vocabulary.**
Translational nature is invisible at the learning level.

### R7 — V/F failure-mode refinement (load-bearing for future sessions)

A more rigorous V/F characterization landed in §2 and §3 of
`syllabus.ontology.md`. Key claims to honor:

- **Twinning is what makes the bridging activity _thinking_ rather than mere
  process.** Design _thinking_ (with user-twin) vs design _process_ (steps
  without the twin). Same on F's side: computational _thinking_ vs computational
  _process_. §2 carries this sharpening.
- **V failure** = design process without design thinking (cargo-culting
  wireframes, personas, A/B tests; symptoms: can't answer specific user
  questions, can't connect features to user needs, no validation evidence).
- **F failure** = computational process without computational thinking
  (cargo-culting unit tests, patterns, refactoring; symptoms: only black-box not
  glass-box tests; patterns subtly incompatible with language/framework).
- **Twin-failure categories** (ignored / wrong / not-yet-known) STAY and apply
  equally to V and F.
- **The twin / process 2×2** with chapter mappings: pure process / V (Ch3) / F
  (Ch2) / both-twins-state (Ch4 + Ch5).
- **Twin-less processes have value** (evolved guardrails) but are NOT a
  substitute for twinning. They sometimes reach similar outcomes to
  twin-without-process in well-bounded settings; do NOT claim they outperform.
- **Strict process WITH strong twinning is itself a failure mode.** Processes
  are descriptive — like music theory describing what master composers do
  (mentioned in passing only, not developed).
- **Documentation as a both-hats case**: requires V (reader twinning) AND F
  (NM-twinning at the documented software's NM-level). Building an API/library
  is creating a new NM layer.
- **Symptoms ≠ failure modes**: ceremony-without-twin and
  design-thinking-process-without-user-twin are what failures look like from
  outside; the failure is upstream, in the twin.

When writing or revising V/F content: name the _thinking_ register when you mean
the twin-grounded practice; name _process_ when you mean the unmoored steps. The
distinction is load-bearing.
